**Quirks to remember in plans:**
- **Landlocked** — every import/export crosses India (mainly) or China; logistics cost is structurally higher
- **Time zone is 45 minutes** — scheduling with US/EU is harder than with India
- **Two calendars** (B.S. + A.D.) — fiscal year is mid-year; tax filings, fiscal planning differ
- **Federal structure** — many registrations are now at the **local (palika) level**; check both federal and provincial rules
- **Power** — load-shedding largely resolved but industrial demand is constrained; backup (gen/inverter) still common
- **Banking hours** — Sun–Thu (sometimes Fri half-day); NRB is the regulator
- **Language** — all official docs can be Nepali; private sector is English-friendly for tech/services

| **T2 — Reputable secondary** | World Bank Nepal, ADB Nepal, IFC Nepal, IMF Nepal, UNDP Nepal, FNCCI, CNI (Confederation of Nepalese Industries), NMA, NCell/NTC reports, NRB quarterly bulletins, reputable Nepali outlets (The Kathmandu Post, The Himalayan Times, Republica, Nepali Times, Onlinekhabar, Setopati, Business 360°) | Trends, benchmarks, market context |

**Rule:** every load-bearing market number needs ≥2 sources, ≥1 of which is T1 or T2. A figure from one T3 source is an anecdote, not a market size.

**Nepal-specific gotcha:** macro statistics are **often 1–2 years stale** by the time they're published (the Economic Survey is published ~6 months after fiscal year-end, with data gaps). Always note the **fiscal year** of the data you cite (e.g., "FY 2081/82" = mid-2024 to mid-2025 A.D.).

## TAM/SAM/SOM for Nepal

**Nepal is a small market (~30M pop, ~$40–45B GDP).** Most global TAM templates overstate by 10–100×. Calibrate.

- **TAM** — for a product serving urban Nepalis (~5–8M people), TAM is **the entire spending category** (e.g., all restaurant delivery in Nepal).
- **SAM** — the segment you can actually reach. Often **Kathmandu Valley + Pokhara + 2–3 cities** (≈ 4–5M people) for urban products. For ag-tech, SAM is the relevant agro-ecological zone.
- **SOM** — what you can capture in 3 years. Realistic **0.5–3% of SAM** in Nepal's fragmented, cash-heavy, relationship-driven markets. Higher only with strong moat (regulatory license, network effect, monopoly position).

**Sanity check:** if your SOM is >NPR 5B (~USD 38M) in a B2C consumer market outside Kathmandu, you're almost certainly overestimating. **Be brutally honest.**

**Investor / funder expectations (Nepal context):**
- Nepal is **not a venture-scale market** for most consumer plays. Local investors expect **profitability in 18–36 months**, not hyper-growth.
- Sectors where Nepal can produce venture-scale outcomes: **IT/BPO export** (selling globally from Nepal), **remittance-adjacent fintech**, **specialty export** (tea, herbs, handicrafts, premium coffee), **hydropower** (large but capital-intensive, separate ecosystem).
- For local consumer plays, the **right comparison is small/medium business** (SME) — profitability, not unicorns.

**Quick market-sizing references (Nepal, 2026, verify before use):**
- Internet/smartphone users: ~24M+ with internet; ~21M+ smartphone (verify via NTA/MIS report)
- Banked population: ~60–70% (NRB); wallet users: 10M+ (eSewa alone)
- Cross-border e-commerce spend: ~USD 1.2B (2026, mainly from Nepalis buying from Amazon, AliExpress, India)
- Domestic e-commerce GMV: smaller (~USD 200–500M range; fast-growing but fragmented)
- Annual tourist arrivals: ~1M (pre-COVID was ~1.2M; recovering)
- Hydropower installed capacity: ~3,000+ MW (target 30,000 MW by 2035 per government plan)
- IT/BPO export revenue: ~NPR 50–80B (~USD 400–600M); growing
- Remittance inflow: ~USD 9–10B annually (25% of GDP)

## Strategic frameworks

Use the right framework for the question. **Universal frameworks, applied to Nepal context.**

| Question | Framework | When to use in Nepal context |
|---|---|---|
| "What industry should I enter / what are the binding constraints?" | **Porter's Five Forces** | Industry analysis; consider landlocked constraint + import dependence |
| "What does the customer actually hire this to do?" | **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)** | Product/messaging; **account for the festival calendar + remittance cycle** as triggers |
| "Where should I grow next?" | **Ansoff Matrix** | Growth planning; **border trade with India is the easiest "new market"** |
| "Which of my products deserve investment?" | **BCG Matrix** | Portfolio decisions |
| "How do I position vs. competitors?" | **Wardley Maps** | Strategic positioning by evolution stage |
| "What's the wedge?" | **Bowling Alley → Big Slam** | Niche-down: pick one district / one customer type / one use case |
| "What's the moat?" | **7 Powers** (Hamilton Helmer) | Defensibility; in Nepal: **regulatory license, network effect, distribution lock-in** are the strongest moats |

**Porter — Nepal-specific additions:**
- **6th force: Remittance & diaspora dynamics** — Nepalis abroad drive ~25% of GDP; products that capture remittance flow (or target diaspora for the home market) have a structural tailwind
- **6th force: India dependency** — Indian policy changes (border, trade, currency) directly affect Nepal; build for resilience, not just efficiency
- **6th force: Federal government complexity** — 753 palikas + 7 provinces = many regulators; navigate or partner
- **6th force: Load-shedding & power reliability** — for any physical/infrastructure business, power is a 6th force

**JTBD — Nepal-specific triggers:**
- **Festival seasonality**: Dashain (Oct), Tihar (Oct/Nov), Teej, Holi, Lhosar, Eid — many categories spike 2–5× here
- **Monsoon (Jun–Sep)**: agriculture cycles, construction slowdowns, travel patterns
- **Remittance cycles**: peak in Oct–Dec (post-migrant rotations), lower in Apr–Jun
- **Wedding season** (Nov–Feb, peak in Magh): jewelry, clothing, venues, photography spike
- **Academic calendar**: school admissions Mar–Apr; supplies Jul–Aug
- **Wedding, rice-planting, harvest** triggers for ag products

**Wardley Maps (Nepal-specific):**
- Most Nepal businesses operate in **"custom-built"** quadrant (every hotel, every SaaS for local retail, every kirana store tool)
- **Opportunity**: move categories from custom → product (e.g., POS, accounting, HR — repeated pain across many businesses)
- **AI substitution risk**: lowest in fields requiring physical presence (agriculture, construction, healthcare delivery); highest in admin, customer service, basic content

## Decision frameworks

| Framework | When | Formula / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Weighted Decision Matrix (Pugh)** | Choose between 3+ options | Criteria × weights; score each option. **For Nepal, add "regulatory/license risk" and "remittance/fx exposure" as criteria.** |
| **RICE** | Feature prioritization | `Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort`. Reach is smaller in Nepal; weight accordingly. |
| **WSJF** | Sequencing work | `Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size` |
| **ICE** | Quick scoring | Impact × Confidence × Ease |
| **Hypothesis Tree** | Diagnose why a metric dropped | Branch from metric to causes |
| **Expected Value (NPR)** | Decisions under uncertainty | `Σ (probability × payoff)` — convert to NPR; account for fx risk |
| **Payback in months** | Capex decisions | `Capex / monthly net profit` — Nepal SMEs often target 12–24 months payback |

**Nepal rule of thumb:** spend **more** time on the decision if the option requires **regulatory license, government relationship, or significant capex** (these have high irreversibility).

## Hypothesis-driven validation

**State the assumption, the test, and the pass/fail metric BEFORE building anything.**

```
Hypothesis:  "Urban Kathmandu millennials will pay NPR 500/mo for a Nepali-language AI study tutor"
Test:        "Landing page + ad spend NPR 50K on Facebook/Instagram; collect 200 signups; convert 30 to paid"
Pass:        ≥15% landing → trial; ≥30% trial → paid; NPS ≥40; weekly retention ≥60%
Fail:        Below any → reposition (different ICP, different promise, different price)
Timeline:    4 weeks
Cost:        NPR 50K ads + 80 hours work
```

**Strong opinions, loosely held:** write the hypothesis with a *directional* prediction, not "we'll see." Design the **cheapest test that could disprove it**.

**Nepal-specific validation ladder (cheapest → most expensive):**
1. **Personal network** (5–10 friends/family) — "would you use this?"
2. **WhatsApp group test** — share a one-pager in 3–5 relevant groups, gauge interest
3. **Smoke test landing page** — NPR 5–10K in Facebook/Instagram ads
4. **Concierge MVP** — manually deliver the service to 5–10 customers
5. **Wizard-of-Oz** — automation is fake; humans do the work
6. **Prototype** — build the smallest real thing
7. **Full product** — only after 4–5 of the above are positive

**Important validation nuances for Nepal:**
- **Trust is built in person.** A WhatsApp intro from a known contact converts 10× better than cold outreach.
- **Word-of-mouth compounds fast** in tight communities (e.g., Newar community in Kathmandu, Marwari business networks, professional associations).
- **English vs. Nepali** — test language preference by segment. Many urban youth prefer English for tech; rural + older prefer Nepali.
- **Price sensitivity is high** — even a small price (NPR 100) can deter if value isn't proven. Anchor to something tangible.

## Pricing in Nepal

**Currency:** all prices in NPR; cross-reference USD only for export or imported-cost analysis.

**Nepal price anchors (2026, urban Kathmandu — verify with FNCCI/consumer surveys):**
- **Chai** (cup, local): NPR 30–80
- **Meal (thali bhojan)**: NPR 250–600
- **Monthly groceries (family of 4)**: NPR 25,000–40,000
- **1BR rent in Kathmandu (avg)**: NPR 15,000–30,000
- **Petrol**: ~NPR 180–200/liter
- **Movie ticket**: NPR 350–600
- **Smartphone (mid-range)**: NPR 25,000–50,000
- **Laptop (mid-range)**: NPR 80,000–150,000
- **Mid-range restaurant dinner for 2**: NPR 2,000–4,000
- **Monthly gym membership**: NPR 2,500–6,000

**Subscription price ceilings (consumer willingness-to-pay estimates, 2026):**
- **Casual consumer (NPR 100–500/mo)** — gaming, entertainment, premium social
- **Engaged consumer (NPR 500–2,000/mo)** — productivity, learning, niche content
- **Premium consumer (NPR 2,000–5,000+/mo)** — only for clear, demonstrated value (Netflix-tier, premium fitness, B2B)
- **B2B SaaS — SME (NPR 1,000–10,000/mo per seat)** — POS, accounting, CRM
- **B2B SaaS — enterprise (NPR 50,000–500,000+/yr)** — HR, ERP, custom
- **B2B services (per project)**: NPR 100K–5M typical for SME digital projects

**Pricing principles (Nepal):**
- **Anchor in tangible value, not abstract features.** "Save 2 hours/day" beats "AI-powered insights"
- **Annual prepay discount 15–25%** is standard; monthly is harder to collect
- **Accept Khalti/eSewa/ConnectIPS/card/cash/bank transfer** — payment friction is real
- **Free tier is expensive to maintain**; freemium rarely works in Nepal (cost of free users > their future LTV in most cases)
- **Test pricing privately** — raise for new customers, grandfather existing
- **Festival discounts work** (Dashain, Tihar) — plan campaigns around them
- **Group/family plans** convert well in tight communities
- **The first NPR 100 is the hardest** — pilot the price with friends at 50% off to build testimonials, then raise

**Pricing models applicable in Nepal:**
| Model | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| One-time purchase | Physical goods, courses | Cash-friendly |
| Monthly subscription | SaaS, content | Works with digital payment rails |
| Annual subscription | SaaS, premium content | Better cash flow, lower churn |
| Freemium | Apps with viral loops | Free tier cost can be prohibitive |
| Pay-per-use (metered) | API, AI inference, SMS | Growing with fintech adoption |
| Tiered (Basic / Pro / Enterprise) | B2B SaaS | Standard |
| Bundle / package | Telecom, DTH, FMCG | Common |

## Payment stack

**Nepal's digital payment ecosystem (2026) — pick the right rails for your product.**

| Payment method | Best for | Notes (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **eSewa** | Consumer-to-business, B2C subscriptions | Largest wallet; 10M+ users; F1Soft-owned. Strong consumer trust. |
| **Khalti** | Developer-friendly integration, B2C | Janaki Technology-owned; strong APIs and merchant onboarding. |
| **IME Pay** | Remittance-linked, cross-border | IME Group; strong in remittance + merchant payments. |
| **ConnectIPS** | Bank-to-bank instant (P2P, P2B) | NRB-backed real-time rail; works through bank apps. |
| **Fonepay** | Bank-to-bank interbank | Largest bank consortium; interoperable. |
| **Cards (Visa/MC)** | E-commerce, premium | Growing but <5% of retail transactions; cross-border is the main use. |
| **Cash on delivery (COD)** | E-commerce (default) | Still dominates domestic e-commerce (~70%+ of orders). |
| **Bank transfer / cheque** | B2B, large amounts | Slow but universal; many B2B still use this. |
| **Mobile banking apps (NMB, NBL, NCell Money, etc.)** | Banked customers | NRB-licensed; all major banks have apps. |
| **QR payments (Fonepay QR, eSewa QR, Khalti QR)** | In-person retail | Standardizing on Fonepay QR for interoperability. |

**Integration recommendations for a Nepal product:**
1. **Consumer B2C:** integrate **eSewa + Khalti** first (covers >80% of wallet users); add **ConnectIPS** for bank-account payers; add **COD** for ecommerce
2. **B2B SaaS:** integrate **ConnectIPS** + **eSewa** + **bank transfer** + cheque for older B2B; invoices via email
3. **Marketplace / ecommerce:** **eSewa + Khalti + COD** (mandatory); add **Fonepay QR** for in-store
4. **Remittance-adjacent:** IME Pay, Western Union, MoneyGram partnerships, official remit licenses

**Nepal-specific payment gotchas:**
- **Refund support varies** by gateway; confirm in writing
- **Settlement time** is T+1 to T+3 for most wallets; bank settlement can be T+1 to T+2
- **PCI-DSS** is not enforced like in US/EU; NRB has its own payment-service provider licensing
- **Cross-border** — Nepal has a **negative list** for imports; you may need LC, bank guarantees, or specific licenses to pay foreign vendors
- **Foreign currency** — NPR is not freely convertible for capital account; foreign investment via FDI channels is regulated

## Nepal compliance checklist

**Don't skip this — Nepal has specific, often-changing regulations.**

| Area | What to know | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Company registration** | Private Ltd (Pvt. Ltd.) is the standard. Register at **Department of Industry (DoI)** for industry, **Company Registrar's Office (CRO)** for services. Name search → PAN → registration. ~NPR 10K–30K + legal fees. | DoI / CRO |
| **PAN (Permanent Account Number)** | Required for all businesses. Apply at **IRD** (Inland Revenue Department). Free; takes days. | IRD |
| **VAT registration** | Mandatory if turnover >NPR 5M (or 2M for goods). Current VAT rate **13%**. File monthly/quarterly returns. | IRD |
| **Income tax / corporate tax** | Resident company: **25%** standard (different for special industries). File annual return. | IRD |
| **TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)** | Must deduct on specified payments (rent, professional fees, etc.) and remit to IRD. | IRD |
| **Labor compliance** | **Labor Act 2074 (2017)** governs: working hours, leave, gratuity, severance, provident fund. Minimum wage revised periodically. | Ministry of Labor |
| **Foreign Investment** | **Foreign Investment Act 2019**. Some sectors **closed** to FDI (cottage, small); some require minimum capital (often NPR 50M+). Apply at DoI. | DoI + NRB (for forex) |
| **Intellectual property** | Trademark, patent, design via **Department of Industry/IP Section**. Nepal IP law is still developing; trademark registration is the most common step. | DoI |
| **Import/Export license** | Required for most cross-border trade. Customs code, IEC (Import Export Code) at Department of Customs. | DoC |
| **Data protection** | **Individual Privacy Act 2075 (2018)** is the core law; **Cyber Security Act 2081 (2024/25)** added cybercrime and platform rules (controversial — review). | Ministry of Communications |
| **Sector-specific licenses** | Tourism (Nepal Tourism Board + DOT), Food (DFTQC), Pharmacy (DDA), Education (Ministry), Finance (NRB), Telecom (NTA), etc. | Respective ministry |
| **Banking/payment** | **NRB licensing** required to operate a payment service, wallet, or remit. Strict KYC/AML. | NRB |
| **Local government** | Many businesses need **local palika (municipality) registration** + business tax in addition to federal. | Palika |

**Minimum viable compliance for a new digital business in Nepal:**
1. Register Pvt. Ltd. at CRO (services) or DoI (manufacturing/industry)
2. Get PAN
3. Register for VAT if applicable
4. Open a business bank account (NMB, NBL, NIC Asia, etc.)
5. Apply for relevant sector-specific licenses
6. Set up TDS + payroll compliance
7. Get a basic trademark registered
8. **For a foreign investor:** get **Foreign Investment Approval** before capital infusion; **NRB approval** for forex inflow

**Sectors to avoid (as a startup, not as a strategic investor):**
- **Pure commodity trading** — margin is thin and Indian competition is fierce

- **Government contracting as core** — high margin but very high-touch and relationship-dependent

## Translating global benchmarks to Nepal

**Global SaaS / venture metrics don't apply directly to Nepal.** Adjustments:

| Global benchmark | Nepal translation |
|---|---|
| TAM $1B+ | SOM in NPR; $1B SAM is unrealistic for most local plays |
| CAC payback 12 months | Often 18–36 months acceptable; track NPR not USD |
| LTV:CAC 3:1 | Often 2:1 acceptable; churn is harder to reduce in price-sensitive market |
| Rule of 40 | Less relevant; aim for **profitability in 18–36 months** instead |
| ARR growth 100% YoY | 50–100% YoY is excellent; most local investors value profit over growth |
| Burn multiple 1× | Most Nepali startups shouldn't burn at all; **path to profit is the metric** |
| "Default alive" | Aim for "default profitable" — no investor? keep running |
| $5M seed round | **NPR 5–20M** (~$37K–150K) is the local seed norm |
| $50M Series A | **NPR 200M–1B** is the local growth-stage norm (and rare) |

**In short:** think in **NPR, months-to-profitability, and relationship-driven distribution** before reaching for global playbooks.

## Lead generation (Nepal B2B)

1. **Define ICP tightly** — "SME in Kathmandu" is not an ICP. "Kathmandu Valley retail businesses with 5–50 staff using Khalti/eSewa, in New Road / New Baneshwor / Pokhara" is.
2. **Build a list** — FNCCI member directory, NIC member directory, LinkedIn (Nepal is small enough to manually research), trade association lists, Yellow Pages Nepal.
3. **Outreach channels (Nepal, ranked by ROI):**
   - **Personal intro / warm referral** — highest conversion
   - **WhatsApp** (Business) — primary business comms; **far more important than email** in Nepal
   - **LinkedIn** — for English-speaking professional segment
   - **Viber** — still common
   - **Facebook/Instagram ads** — works for B2C, weaker for B2B
   - **Industry events** — FNCCI, NMA, CMA events; trade fairs
   - **Cold call / walk-in** — works for B2B in some industries
   - **Email** — works for enterprise and IT; lower open rates than global
4. **Multi-touch sequence:** WhatsApp → call → LinkedIn → in-person meeting; **in-person matters more than in US/EU**
5. **Nepal-specific trust builders:**
   - Physical office in a known area
   - Personal Facebook page of the founder
   - Recommendations from a known community figure
   - Nepali language website (even for B2B)
   - Bank reference / NRB license (if financial)
   - Photos with real customers (not stock)

## Common mistakes (global) to avoid in Nepal

- ❌ Fabricate market data
- ❌ Mix evidence tiers without labelling
- ❌ Use only top-down TAM (do bottom-up first)
- ❌ Confuse "growth" with "PMF" (Sean Ellis ≥40% very disappointed; retention curve shape)
- ❌ Build features without a hypothesis
- ❌ Apply US/EU SaaS benchmarks to Nepal consumer market
- ❌ Skip compliance (PAN, VAT, TDS, Labor Act) — penalties are real
- ❌ Underestimate relationship-driven distribution (cold outreach alone rarely works)
- ❌ Build for English-only audience (Nepali language unlocks 10× the market)
- ❌ Use Stripe / global gateways (use eSewa/Khalti/ConnectIPS instead)
- ❌ Ignore monsoon / festival seasonality
- ❌ Hire before product-market fit (use contractors, not full-time)
- ❌ Raise venture money when you should bootstrap (most Nepal plays should bootstrap to profitability)
- ❌ Skip the local palika — they matter

## Nepal-specific anti-patterns

These fail here but work elsewhere:

- ❌ **USD-denominated pricing** — Nepalis think in NPR; a "$10/mo" product reads as "premium"; a "NPR 1,299/mo" reads as "1,300"
- ❌ **Annual-only subscriptions** — monthly is the local norm; annual is 2nd-year
- ❌ **Heavyweight B2B sales motion for SME** — owners are busy; sell through WhatsApp + a single call, not 4 meetings
- ❌ **Cloud-only with no offline mode** — connectivity is patchy in many areas
- ❌ **Assume everyone has a credit card** — most don't; integrate wallets first
- ❌ **English-only support** — Nepali support is table stakes for mass market
- ❌ **Long onboarding forms** — every field reduces conversion 10%; use minimal KYC
- ❌ **Heavy KYC at signup** — gate KYC by usage tier; progressive disclosure
- ❌ **Pan-India / global shipping model** — Nepal is landlocked; logistics is a real cost
- ❌ **Treat Kathmandu as "Nepal"** — Kathmandu is 5% of the population; Pokhara, Biratnagar, Butwal, Nepalgunj, Dhangadhi are different markets with different needs
- ❌ **Ignore province & district-level variation** — Madhesh vs Hill vs Mountain have very different consumer behavior
- ❌ **Assume festival demand is universal** — Hindu festivals (Dashain, Tihar) vs Buddhist (Loshar) vs Muslim (Eid) vary by region
- ❌ **Build a "Nepali version of [global app]"** — usually a feature gap, not a product gap. Find a unique local wedge.
- ❌ **Skip the offline/paper world** — many SMEs still use paper; tools that bridge paper ↔ digital (OCR, simple apps) are gold
- ❌ **Assume everyone uses email** — WhatsApp is the inbox; design for it

## Public site scraping & downloads

> **Goal:** when researching a specific topic, vendor, policy, or sector, **download the relevant public material locally** for offline review, archival, and reuse. This is essential in Nepal where connectivity varies and some sites go down.

### When to scrape (vs. just read)

**Scrape + save locally when:**
- The site has multiple relevant pages (e.g., SEBON notices archive)
- You need to compare items across time (e.g., NRB circulars by month)
- The content may be removed (gazette notices, old news)
- You're building a report that needs to cite specific pages
- You want offline access for further analysis

**Just read (no save) when:**
- One-off look-up
- Content is stable and public (e.g., Wikipedia)
- You'd be violating ToS (some sites prohibit scraping; respect this)

### Scraping discipline (always)

1. **Check ToS / robots.txt** first — if forbidden, ask before scraping
2. **Use rate limiting** — max 1 request/sec, 1 request/2sec for politeness
3. **Identify yourself** with a real User-Agent (`Mozilla/5.0 ... (research; contact: ...)`)
4. **Cache** — don't refetch the same URL within 24h
5. **Preserve source URL + date accessed** in every saved file
6. **Save both raw HTML and extracted text** (HTML is for re-parse; text is for analysis)

### Code Mode recipe (full pattern)

```javascript
// Step 1: Discover URLs to scrape
const page = await tools.fetch.fetch_url({url: "https://sebon.gov.np/category/notices/", max_length: 30000});
const links = (page.match(/href="([^"]*\/(notices|circulars)\/[^"]+)"/g) || []).slice(0, 20);

// Step 2: For each URL, fetch and save
const today = new Date().toISOString().slice(0,10);
const subdir = `sebon-notices`;
for (const link of links) {
  const url = link.replace('href="', '').replace('"', '');
  const fullUrl = new URL(url, "https://sebon.gov.np").toString();
  const html = await tools.fetch.fetch_url({url: fullUrl, max_length: 50000});
  const slug = fullUrl.split("/").filter(Boolean).pop() || "index";
  // Save raw HTML
  await tools.filesystem.write_file({
    path: `~/Downloads/BusinessPlanning/${subdir}/${today}/${slug}.html`,
    content: html
  });
  // (Optionally) extract text and save
  const text = html.replace(/<[^>]+>/g, " ").replace(/\s+/g, " ");
  await tools.filesystem.write_file({
    path: `~/Downloads/BusinessPlanning/${subdir}/${today}/${slug}.txt`,
    content: text
  });
  // Rate limit
  await new Promise(r => setTimeout(r, 1500));
}
```

## Tech & service transfer

> **Goal:** identify products, services, and business models that are **proven elsewhere** (India, SEA, US, Europe, China) and **could work in Nepal** with localization. Build a pipeline of importable ideas.

| **Pakistan** (TechJuice, ProPakistani) | Cash-on-delivery, mobile wallets, Islamic finance | Muslim + Hindu + Buddhist mix, COD, mobile-first |

| **LatAm (Brazil, Mexico)** | Pix-style instant payment, super-app | ConnectIPS analog |
| **China** (36Kr, PingWest) | Super-app, livestream commerce, group buying | Remittance + diaspora + cross-border (China border) |
| **US/EU** | B2B SaaS, productivity, niche verticals | IT/BPO export opportunity — sell globally from Nepal |

### How to identify transferable ideas

Use this filter (Nepal-fit score):

1. **Wedge existence** — does the idea solve a real Nepal problem (not just "Nepali version of X")? (1–5)
2. **Price sensitivity fit** — can the unit economics work at NPR price points? (1–5)
3. **Regulatory fit** — is it allowed? (Foreign investment, data, sector license) (1–5)
4. **Distribution fit** — can it be sold via WhatsApp + personal network in Nepal? (1–5)
5. **Talent fit** — can Nepal find/afford the talent? (1–5)
6. **Time to first revenue** — < 6 months? (1–5)
7. **Capex requirement** — < NPR 5M? (1–5)
8. **Defensibility** — what stops someone from copying you in 6 months? (1–5)

**Score ≥ 25/40 = strong candidate.** Below 20 = skip.

### Transfer playbook (concrete recipe)

```
1. SOURCE:           Identify idea from overseas source (1 idea/week target)
2. DEEP-DIVE:        Read their launch story, growth tactics, unit economics
3. NEPAL FIT:        Score with the 8-dimension framework above
4. COMPETITOR SCAN:  Who in Nepal does this already? What's their share?
5. REGULATORY CHECK: What licenses/approvals are needed?
6. PRICE TEST:       Build a landing page; run NPR 5–10K ads; measure interest
7. PARTNER SCAN:     Are there diaspora/overseas partners to bring this in?
8. LOCALIZE:         Translate, adjust pricing, add Nepali payment, build trust
9. LAUNCH:           Concierge MVP → paid pilot → full product
```

## Workflow playbook

> **The end-to-end recipe: "I have an idea (or saw a news item) — how do I turn it into a business plan?"**

### Phase 1: Capture (always)
- See a news item, policy change, or overseas idea → save to `~/Downloads/BusinessPlanning/news-intel/{date}/` immediately
- Tag with `#opportunity` in the filename or frontmatter
- Set a 7-day reminder to revisit

### Phase 2: Triage (within 7 days)
- 5-minute score with the **Nepal-fit 8-dimension framework**
- ≥25/40 = advance to research; 20–25 = hold; <20 = drop
- Update the master `_index.md` in `~/Downloads/BusinessPlanning/`

### Phase 3: Research (1–2 weeks)
- Use `deep-research` skill to gather context (SearXNG, fetch, playwright)
- Download all relevant pages to `~/Downloads/BusinessPlanning/{topic}/{date}/`
- Validate with **Nepal T1/T2 sources** first
- Talk to 5–10 potential customers (personal network, WhatsApp groups)

### Phase 4: Synthesize (1–3 days)
- Write the report to `~/Work/Zurnel/Reports/Business/{topic}/{YYYY-MM-DD}-{slug}.md`
- Use the **Reports** structure (executive summary, findings, evidence, synthesis, sources)
- Include: opportunity thesis, Nepal fit score, competitive scan, regulatory checklist, financial sketch (NPR), risks
- Link from the relevant `_index.md`

### Phase 5: Decide (1 day)
- **Hypothesis-driven validation**: write the test, the metric, the pass/fail threshold
- **Payback in months** — is this <24 months? If not, reconsider
- Decision matrix (Pugh) if comparing 2+ opportunities
- **Commit to 1** or **park** with a re-check date

### Phase 6: Pilot (2–8 weeks)
- **Concierge / Wizard-of-Oz** — don't build tech; deliver manually
- Find 3–10 paying customers
- Use the feedback to refine pricing, positioning, delivery
- Save learnings to `~/Work/Zurnel/Reports/Business/{topic}/pilot-results.md`

### Phase 7: Build (if validated)
- Now you can build the product (use `development-workflows` skill)
- Compliance: register company, get PAN, VAT, license
- Hire: first hire should be for sales/delivery, not engineering
- Set up: payment gateway (eSewa/Khalti), basic CRM (HubSpot free or local), accounting (Tally or local)

### Phase 8: Scale (if profitable)
- Hire second, third
- Reinvest profits
- Consider funding only if unit economics are proven
- Build defensibility (brand, network, regulatory license, switching cost)

## Final note

**Nepal is a market of relationships, festivals, and resilience.** Frameworks help, but the people you trust, the festivals you plan around, and the willingness to operate through monsoon load-shedding and political uncertainty are the real moats.
